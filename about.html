Welcome to The 1,200 Kitchen

Small portions. Big flavour. Meals that actually satisfy.

The idea is simple. Food should be enjoyable, portions should feel realistic, and knowing the nutritional value of a meal shouldn’t mean sacrificing flavour.

Most recipe sites treat nutrition as an afterthought — a table buried below forty paragraphs about the author’s holiday. Most calorie-counting apps treat food as arithmetic. This site is an attempt at the thing in between: proper recipes, written to be cooked and enjoyed, with the numbers where you can actually see them.

What the recipes are built around

  • Everyday ingredients. Things you can buy in a normal shop, and that you will use again rather than buying once for a single recipe.
  • Manageable portions. Sized honestly, and described so you know what one actually looks like.
  • Meals that satisfy. Built with volume, protein, fibre and texture, because those are what make a modest portion feel like dinner.
  • Clear calories and macros. On the card, on the recipe page, in the meal planner. Never hidden.
  • Simple preparation. Most of these are a pan and a bowl.
  • Flexibility. Scale a recipe up or down and the per-serving nutrition stays the same.
  • Mixing and matching. Every recipe is designed to be a component of a day, not a fixed plan you follow.

About the 1,200

The name is where the collection started. It is the inspiration behind the recipes and the branding, and it is a useful anchor for the kind of cooking this is: modest portions that still feel like real food.

1,200 is a starting point for a recipe collection, not a recommendation for a person.

It is worth being direct about this, because the number appears in the name and names carry weight. Individual calorie needs differ enormously — by age, by height, by weight, by how much you move, and by things no equation captures. For a great many adults, 1,200 kcal a day is meaningfully below what their body actually needs. It is not a default and it is not a goal.

So the site is built the other way round. The default target in the meal planner is 1,200 because the collection has to start somewhere, but you can change it to anything, and the calorie calculator exists so that you have your own figure to work from rather than borrowing the one in the title. If you do one thing on this site before building a day, make it that.

The calculator will also decline to hand you a very low target. If the arithmetic lands somewhere unusually low, it stops and suggests a conversation with a healthcare professional instead. Something that low is worth proper support, and a website is not the right place to get it.

How to use the site

  1. Work out your estimated needs, so the numbers that follow mean something for you.
  2. Browse the recipes, or start from what is already in your fridge.
  3. Build a day and watch the totals fill up. When there is room left, the site suggests things that fit it.
  4. Generate a grocery list from whatever you chose. Ingredients from different recipes are combined into single lines.

Nothing needs an account. Your target, your day, your list and your fridge are saved in your own browser and stay there.

On the numbers

Recipes are marked verified when the figures come from actual product labels, and estimated when they are built from generic ingredient values. Estimated figures carry a tilde and are rounded, because inventing precision would be worse than admitting the uncertainty. There is more about this on the nutrition page.
